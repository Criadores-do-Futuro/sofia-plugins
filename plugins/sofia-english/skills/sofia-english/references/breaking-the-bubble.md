# Breaking into a bubble

Sofia's goal is to write scripts that break into a bubble, so the video has a better chance of going viral. Breaking into a bubble means getting the algorithm to deliver the video to a group of people who are super interested in one subject. This piece brings together what the method observes about bubbles, theme, commander's intent and comments, and shows how each one enters the script.

The creators and videos named here are cases the method studied (most of them Brazilian, kept as they happened). They exist to show the mechanism. Never copy them, and none of their numbers becomes a promise to the creator.

## 1. What a bubble is

A bubble is a group of people who are super interested in one specific subject.

- **A bubble and a niche are different things.** A niche is a broad professional or commercial segment, like marketing, nutrition or fitness. A bubble grows out of genuine interest and engagement around a tightly focused subject. Nutrition is a niche. Dachshund puppies are a bubble.
- **Bubbles change over time.** A bubble is born, grows fast, then shrinks or disappears, following the attention of culture and media. Chess is the example: the bubble exploded when the series The Queen's Gambit went viral, then settled down.
- **Bubble and theme go together.** The theme is what the video is about. The bubble is the people who are super interested in it. Choosing the theme is choosing which bubble the video will travel in.

### The quick test

A bubble has the name of a subject or a community: what these people love, follow, argue about or live. If the name describes a state, a pain or a difficulty of the audience, it describes the target audience or the pain, and the bubble has not been found yet.

- "People who don't know how to use their iPhone's features" describes a difficulty. Nobody gathers around not knowing something. The bubble there is iPhone: the people who follow phone tips.
- Check question: are there people who look up this subject for fun, comment on it, argue about it and send it to friends? If so, it's a bubble.

### The 6 types of bubble

1. **Of the moment (media):** hot subjects of the week or month that are in the public debate, like a fight over work schedules, a reality show, a series premiere or a public feud.
2. **Universal:** dilemmas every human being faces, which never get old, like relationships, family, the brief nature of life (and what comes with it, like the quarter-life or thirties crisis), rejection, purpose and injustice.
3. **Ideological:** subjects with strong polarization and deep conviction, like politics (for example, the arguments around a country's leading politicians), religion, veganism and feminism.
4. **Creator:** when one person locks in a format and becomes the center of interest. The method's Brazilian cases: Felipe Neto, Rodrigo Pantera, Jenny Keller, Rafael Gratta, Mari Krieger, Nina Talks (Nina Baiocchi) and Hanna Franklin.
5. **Random interest:** specific passions and subjects, like specialty coffee, dachshunds, black cats, crochet, soccer, astrology signs, pimple popping, typefaces, K-dramas, hip-hop, Linkin Park and chess.
6. **Niche and profession:** professional and market segments, like nutrition (or prenatal nutrition), weightlifting, digital marketing, paid ads and social media management.

### Bubble and target audience

They are two roles in the same video.

- The **bubble** is who the algorithm will find and deliver the video to. It lives in the first circle, the theme.
- The **target audience** ("Who it's for" in the video brief) is who the creator wants as a client, who hires or trusts them. It lives in the second circle, commander's intent.

In a niche bubble the two can overlap. When they don't, closeness to the goal (step 1 in section 8) tells whether the bubble is worth it.

## 2. How the algorithm finds the bubble

The algorithm doesn't hate the creator. It serves how the human mind behaves and looks for groups of super-interested people to deliver what they will love most.

1. **Initial distribution:** the posted video goes to a sample of people (for example, a thousand).
2. **Looking for groups:** the algorithm checks whether some specific group watches longer and interacts more.
3. **Watch time:** when it finds a group where watch time and engagement shoot up, it recognizes a bubble. In one of the tests the method studied, watch time rose from 25 to 35 seconds and likes went from 5 thousand to more than 100 thousand.
4. **Mass distribution:** once the response is validated, the video goes almost only to people in that bubble, and reach grows.

That's why the script speaks a bubble's language from the opening: that response is what the algorithm measures.

## 3. The 3 pillars and the 90/10 rule

1. **Creative format (90% of the effort):** the visual and script structure, stable and repeatable, that makes sure the video works.
2. **Bubble (reach vehicle):** the theme that attracts attention and brings 10 times more reach.
3. **Commander's intent (sales seed):** the idea planted in the video to generate sales in the future.

Bubble and commander's intent are the 10%: pieces you swap inside a format that's already stable. In practice, first comes a format that works ([formats](formats/README.md)). Then, video after video, you swap the bubble and the commander's intent.

## 4. Theme, the first circle

- **What it is:** the specific subject of the video. It's the front door, the hook that pulls attention and decides which bubble the video travels in.
- **Why it matters:** the theme is the reach vehicle. Talking only about the service or the technical product doesn't catch the internet's attention. A hot theme, or one that never gets old, draws eyes, and then there's room to plant the sales seed.
- **How to choose:** by familiarity (a subject the person already lives, masters or enjoys: hobbies, series, stories) and by closeness to the goal (a theme that connects with what they sell).
- **How to understand it deeply:** a theme is full of subtext and subtopics. "Relationships" or "the brief nature of life" hold everyday dilemmas, the quarter-life or thirties crisis, jealousy, fighting in public, the move into adult life and values like simplicity. Each subtopic is a different video in the same bubble.
- **How to research it:** in the TikTok search bar. A theme has no official account (there is no @relationships), so search is the way in. Type the exact theme (relationships, cheating, coffee) and look at the videos that went most viral. Then spend about 30 minutes watching 3 to 5 videos on the theme to the end, liking and sharing. That teaches the platform the person belongs to the group and fills their feed with its videos. On Instagram, the feed trains the same way.

In [theme and moral](addictive-elements/theme-and-moral.md), the theme is the vehicle and the moral is the cargo.

## 5. Commander's intent, the second circle

Commander's intent is the sales seed: the idea planted in the video to generate sales, positioning or authority in the future. In [theme and moral](addictive-elements/theme-and-moral.md), it's the moral, the idea the creator wants to sell to their audience. The target audience ("Who it's for" in the video brief) lives in this circle.

The three pillars (Authenticity, Movement and Product), how to build each one with the creator, where to keep them and how the seed enters the script, with the hidden CTA and the direct CTA, are in [commander's intent](commanders-intent.md).

## 6. The two forces that break bubbles

To get into any group, the video triggers at least one of these forces:

- **Identification:** the "this is about me" or "I've lived this" trigger. It comes from strong beliefs, shared hatreds, everyday situations and the group's own vocabulary. See [familiar language](addictive-elements/familiar-language.md) and [emotional relevance](addictive-elements/emotional-relevance.md).
- **Conflict:** the friction between desire and reality (what the person wishes were true versus what actually is), or a deliberate collision with a sacred belief of that bubble. See [conflict and change](addictive-elements/conflict-and-change.md).

In the case the method studied that collides with a sacred belief (Pantera's, in section 9), the provocation opens the video and right after it the story turns to praise the creator.

## 7. The creator's eye (the Matrix Lens)

An active creator doesn't watch videos passively. They read the hidden codes: the method calls this the Matrix Lens. Facing a video that went viral, they ask:

1. Which bubble is this video breaking into?
2. Which format did it use?
3. Which commander's intent was planted?

Sofia asks the same three questions when she reads a reference or a video from the creator's profile.

## 8. The method in 3 steps

### Step 1: choose the bubble on purpose

The first choice doesn't aim at an immediate sale. It aims at mastering the mechanism of breaking into a bubble. Two criteria:

- **Familiarity (the priority to start):** a bubble the person already lives in day to day, as a private person and not only as a work niche: hobbies, series (K-dramas), bands (Linkin Park), sports, animals. Whoever lives in that world already knows the slang, the inside jokes, the dilemmas and the open wounds, with no effort.
- **Closeness to the goal:** the connection between the bubble's audience and the product, service or authority the person is building.

Start with familiar bubbles to master the technique. Once it clicks, move on to more complex bubbles or ones closer to the niche.

For Sofia, a bubble is a long-term project of the profile. Before naming a new one, check the bubbles the creator profile already lists (in the conversation or in `sofia-english/styles/<profile>.md`, when the host has file tools). To choose a new one, ask the creator what they're a fan of or what they live day to day, and only then cross it with what they sell.

### Step 2: active investigation

Before the first line of script, gather the community's emotional raw material.

1. **Train the algorithm.**
   - Theme bubble (specialty coffee, cheating, relationships): the TikTok search bar, about 30 minutes, 3 to 5 videos on the theme watched to the end, liking and sharing.
   - Creator bubble: their profile, or other people's videos talking about them.
2. **Mine the most-liked comments.** Open 10 to 15 videos that went viral in the bubble and go straight to comments with tens of thousands of likes (10 thousand, 20 thousand, 30 thousand). They show what thousands of people think and feel together.
3. **Capture the literal language.** Write down the exact words and phrases, with the audience's slang and way of talking.
4. **Map the two forces.** Separate what is identification from what is conflict.

The numbers are a guide, with no minimum. A small or new bubble may have fewer videos and comments with fewer likes. What matters is taking the most-liked ones that exist and telling the creator where each one came from.

What the investigation finds (vocabulary, emotions, comments with their likes and the link, and references) goes into the video brief in the conversation. Save it to the bubble's section of the creator profile in the chosen folder only when the host has file tools and the creator agrees. The step by step is in [bubble research](bubble-research.md).

### Step 3: the creative intersection in the script

1. **The 90/10 rule:** the script starts from a creative format that's already validated. Bubble and commander's intent are the swapped pieces.
2. **Venn diagram:** two circles, the bubble (the theme) and the commander's intent. The script never forces the two together. Find the common ground between the bubble's subject and what you want to communicate.
3. **Rubik's cube:** turn the possible angles until you find the bridge. An example the method studied: the relationships bubble with a storytelling service. The common ground was change, because relationships demand change and good stories demand the character's transformation.
4. **The slide:** the script in three parts.
   - **Opening (hook with the bubble):** strong conflict or identification, in the mined language.
   - **Transition (the common ground):** the video slides from the bubble's subject to the point of connection, without the viewer noticing the change of subject.
   - **Close (commander's intent and call to action):** delivers the sales or positioning idea and ends with a clear call to action.

The slide fits inside the format's structure: the opening is the hook, the transition happens in the middle of the structure, and the close brings the moral with the call to action.

## 9. Comments, the bubble's voice

- **What they are:** the bubble's real voice and a literal portrait of popular imagination.
- **Why they matter:** nobody has to guess from scratch what a community feels. Comments show the sacred beliefs, shared hatreds, pains and desires. A comment with thousands of likes (10 thousand, 20 thousand, 30 thousand) proves that a lot of people think and feel that way.
- **How to read them:** each comment fits one of the two forces.
  - Identification: "this is about me", "I've lived this", strong conviction, shared hatred.
  - Conflict: friction between desire and reality, or a passionate reaction to a broken expectation.
- **How to research them:** 10 to 15 videos that went viral on the theme or the creator being investigated. Skip random comments and go straight to the most liked.
- **How to keep them:** the exact sentence, with the slang, typos, all caps, punctuation and emoji, without correcting it and without swapping in other words. No @ and no name of whoever wrote it. With the likes and the video link, when available.
- **How to use them in the script:**
  - **Same vocabulary:** the same language as the comments creates an instant connection. The viewer thinks: "I think that, and in these exact words."
  - **Open with conflict or provocation:** a high-impact comment can become the opening line, to challenge the group's belief and stop the scroll. A case the method studied: investigating Rodrigo Pantera, the comment with 31 thousand likes said "ele todo querido saindo com seus 15 kg de sobrecoxa" (roughly, "him all sweet walking out with his 15 kg of chicken thighs"). The script opened with a tone of conflict and disbelief: "É sério que ele tá feliz para comprar 15 kg de sobrecoxa?" ("Is he seriously happy to buy 15 kg of chicken thighs?"). Fans of Pantera's simplicity were instantly outraged and attention stayed locked. Then the story turned to praise his qualities.
  - **Raw material:** the mined phrases become arguments, inside jokes and examples in the body of the script.
- **How a comment enters the text:** always as the creator's own line, without quotes and without metalanguage. The script never says it was a comment: no "in the comments", "someone commented", "some people say", "a lot of people", "people", "followers" or "bubble". The words stay as close to the original as possible. Adjust only tense, agreement and the link to the previous sentence, and remove what can't be said out loud (emoji, link, @). In the opening, the part that grabs stays the same (in Pantera's case, "15 kg of chicken thighs") and the rest becomes the creator's question or provocation.
- **Where a comment goes:** at the point where it makes the most sense. In the opening, when it's about conflict or provocation and has a lot of likes. In the middle, as proof, a turn, an example or a reaction. Tell the creator in the conversation which comment each passage came from, never in the script.

## 10. Breaking into your own niche's bubble

Breaking into your own market's bubble (nutrition, marketing) is harder, because people there reveal less of what they feel than in entertainment subjects. In return, the followers arrive much more ready to buy. Two paths:

1. **Name a big name in the field:** create conflict or curiosity around a well-known authority. A case the method studied: the video "Pedro Sobral copiou meu vídeo" ("Pedro Sobral copied my video"), which brought 30 thousand qualified followers. Naming someone requires a fact with a source. No invented accusation or story about a real person.
2. **Touch a shared open wound:** a real pain or frustration of the profession, without naming names. A case the method studied: the video that opens with "Social media virou pet de empreendedor" ("Social media managers became business owners' pets"), which went viral by touching the undervaluing of the profession.

## 11. Before delivering

- **BB1.** The bubble has the name of a subject or a community, passes the quick test and is one of the 6 types.
- **BB2.** The theme, the commander's intent and the common ground are clear, with no forced connection.
- **BB3.** The opening triggers identification or conflict in the bubble's words.
- **BB4.** The transition reaches the common ground without a change-of-subject cut.
- **BB5.** The close delivers the commander's intent with a clear call to action.
- **BB6.** The comments used are in the audience's words, as the creator's own line, without quotes and without metalanguage.
- **BB7.** A real person's name only appears with a fact that has a source.
- **BB8.** The creative format follows what already works (90/10).
