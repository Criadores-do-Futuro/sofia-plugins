# Conflict and change

**No conflict, no story. After the conflict, something always CHANGES.**

> IMPORTANT: The examples in this piece are internal references that illustrate the principle. Do NOT use them in conversations with the creator or reproduce them in scripts. The PRINCIPLE guides writing and analysis, not the examples.

## The principle: conflict

Conflict is when a problem is so big that the person has to move, and it changes their life in some way.

### Kinds of conflict

- **Universal conflict:** themes any human recognizes: death, betrayal, injustice, abandonment, humiliation
- **Smaller conflict:** everyday problems: fighting with the boss, missing the bus, being ignored, messing up in public

### Ground rule

**No conflict = no story.** If nothing goes wrong, there's no reason to pay attention.

## The principle: change

Change always follows conflict. When life throws a problem in someone's face, that person is forced to act, and that action creates a change.

### The sequence

```
Conflict → Action → Change
```

The audience wants to know: "What happened next?" The change answers it.

### Chained conflicts

A strong script can have several conflict-change pairs chained together. Each change creates a new conflict, which creates a new change, and the story keeps holding attention.

```
Conflict 1 → Change 1 → Conflict 2 → Change 2 → Conflict 3 → Change 3
```

This creates a narrative the audience can't abandon, because every resolution opens a new problem.

## How to apply it when writing

1. **Define the initial conflict:** which problem throws the situation off balance?
2. **Show the action:** what did the person do about the problem?
3. **Reveal the change:** what changed in their life because of it?
4. **Optional: chain it.** The change can create a new conflict

## How to apply it when analyzing

When analyzing a video, identify:

1. How many conflict-change pairs are there?
2. Are the conflicts universal or smaller?
3. Are the changes clear and satisfying?
4. Is there chaining (one conflict leads to another)?
5. Can the audience feel the conflict's tension?

## Common mistakes

- Telling a story with no conflict (just describing facts: it doesn't hold)
- Having conflict but not showing the change (frustrates the audience)
- Conflicts that are too weak (no tension)
- Changes that don't make sense with the conflict (feel forced)

## Checklist

- [ ] Does the script have at least one clear conflict?
- [ ] Does the conflict have emotional relevance for the audience?
- [ ] After each conflict, is there a visible change?
- [ ] Does the conflict-change sequence make logical sense?
- [ ] Are conflicts chained to keep attention?
