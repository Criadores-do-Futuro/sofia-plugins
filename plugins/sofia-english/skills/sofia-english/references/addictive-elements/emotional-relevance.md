# Emotional relevance

**Without emotion there is no attention.**

> IMPORTANT: The examples in this piece are internal references that illustrate the principle. Do NOT use them in conversations with the creator or reproduce them in scripts. The PRINCIPLE guides writing and analysis, not the examples.

## The principle

Everything that holds attention has one thing in common: it makes us feel something.

Anger, curiosity, joy, outrage, hope, fear... some feeling has to be sparked.

**Emotional relevance is choosing a theme that touches a universal feeling, one anyone has lived or recognizes.**

## Why it works

- The human brain prioritizes information that carries emotion
- Emotional content gets reactions in the comments (agreement, disagreement, personal stories)
- Content with emotional relevance doesn't go unnoticed
- Making the audience FEEL is the first step to getting the content to ENGAGE

These are observations of the method, not cited science.

## How it shows up in the comments

When emotional relevance is there, the comments show:

- People sharing personal experiences tied to the theme
- Strong agreement ("Said it all!")
- Strong disagreement ("Completely disagree")
- Emotional reactions (people saying they cried, laughed, got angry)
- Debates between people with opposite views

## How to apply it

1. Pick a theme that touches a feeling your audience knows
2. The first sentence should already spark a feeling
3. It can be any feeling; it doesn't have to be sad or dramatic
4. What matters is that the viewer FEELS something when they hear or read it

## Kinds of emotion that work

- **Outrage:** something the audience finds unfair or wrong
- **Curiosity:** something the audience wants to find out
- **Identification:** something the audience has lived
- **Hope:** something the audience wants to reach
- **Fear:** something the audience wants to avoid
- **Humor:** something that makes the audience laugh or have fun

## Checklist

- [ ] Does the chosen theme touch a universal feeling?
- [ ] Does the first sentence already spark emotion?
- [ ] Does the script keep the emotional tension through the video?
- [ ] Is the chosen emotion relevant to the audience's bubble?
