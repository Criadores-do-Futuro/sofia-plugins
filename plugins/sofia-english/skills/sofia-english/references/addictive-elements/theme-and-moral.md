# Theme and moral

**The two most important elements of any script.**

> IMPORTANT: The examples in this piece are internal references that illustrate the principle. Do NOT use them in conversations with the creator or reproduce them in scripts. The PRINCIPLE guides writing and analysis, not the examples.

## The two concepts

### Theme

**What you use to grab your audience's attention.**

The theme is the way in. It's what makes the viewer stop scrolling and pay attention.

### Moral

**The idea you want to sell to your audience.**

The moral is the destination. It's the message you want to stay in the viewer's head after watching.

## What makes a theme interesting?

Something with **emotional relevance** for your audience (see [emotional relevance](emotional-relevance.md)).

- Without emotion, there is no attention
- The theme has to spark a universal feeling
- Interesting themes are usually universal conflicts or touch strong emotions

### The technical-theme trap

Many creators make the mistake of using the technical subject they want to teach as the theme. But technical themes rarely carry emotional relevance.

**The principle:** use a theme with emotional relevance to LEAD the audience to the moral (which can be the technical content).

### How it works in practice (internal reference)

- Someone wants to teach storytelling → "storytelling" isn't an interesting theme → they use "betrayal" (a universal conflict with lots of emotional relevance) as the theme that leads to the moral
- Someone wants to teach about paying yourself a salary from your business → "owner's draw" has no emotional relevance → they use "being financially supported by a man" (a universal conflict that sparks strong debate) as the theme that leads to the moral

## The relationship between theme and moral

```
THEME (way in)                  →     MORAL (destination)
Emotional relevance                   Your message or idea
Holds attention                       Plants it in the head
The audience stops to listen          The audience leaves thinking
```

The theme is the VEHICLE. The moral is the CARGO.

You use an interesting theme to carry the idea you want to plant.

In [breaking into a bubble](../breaking-the-bubble.md), the same two roles become two circles: the theme is the bubble's circle (the vehicle for reach) and the moral is the commander's intent (the sales seed). The script connects the two through their common ground, without a forced join. How to build the profile's commander's intent in three pillars, and how it enters each script, is in [commander's intent](../commanders-intent.md).

## How to choose a good theme

1. **Does it have emotional relevance?** Does it spark a feeling?
2. **Is it a universal conflict?** Do people hold opposite opinions about it?
3. **Does the audience identify with it?** Have they lived it or do they recognize it?
4. **Does it connect to the moral?** Can you build a bridge from the theme to your message?

## How to define a good moral

1. **What idea do you want to plant?** Be specific
2. **What do you want the viewer to do, think or feel when it ends?**
3. **Does the moral connect naturally with the theme?**
4. **Is the moral clear enough to stick?**

## Common mistakes

- Using the technical subject as the theme (no emotional relevance)
- An interesting theme with no clear moral (entertains but plants nothing)
- A strong moral with a weak theme (nobody stops to listen)
- A moral disconnected from the theme (feels forced)

## Checklist

- [ ] Does the theme have emotional relevance for the audience's bubble?
- [ ] Is the theme different from the moral? (theme ≠ moral)
- [ ] Is the moral clear: which idea will be planted?
- [ ] Is there a logical bridge between theme and moral?
- [ ] Does the theme make the viewer stop? Does the moral make them think?
