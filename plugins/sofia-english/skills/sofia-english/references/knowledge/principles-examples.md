# Virality principles: real examples applied

Use: a library of examples by principle. The scriptwriter and the analyst look here to see each principle embodied in a real case, not just the theory. Copy the MECHANICS, never the text.

Corpus: a principles analysis over a pilot batch (14 videos) plus an enrichment batch (13) plus @alberta.nyc (30, from 2026).

Each principle from [principles](../principles.md) with the **best real example** found in the corpus: the verbatim line, **why** it's that principle, the source (with metrics) and the source's **link**. Examples are for studying the mechanics: **never copy the text**. Lines originally spoken in Portuguese or Spanish are given as English renderings and marked (PT) or (ES); the others are verbatim. A slash ( / ) separates speakers in a dialogue.

> Legend: 🟢 AI niche · 🟡 adjacent · 🔴 outside the niche (mechanics only).

## 1. THEME (sets the ceiling)

### 1a. Universal theme: money (maximum ceiling)
- **Verbatim (PT):** "One habit that, if you start doing it, will change your financial life. Listen to what I'm telling you."
- **Why it's THEME:** money arrives with emotional charge already in place: the video doesn't build the emotion, it only switches it on. Maximum ceiling, it crosses any bubble. Result: 1.0M views and **18.4k reposts** with no CTA at all.
- **Source:** 🔴 @eusouroberth · [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@eusouroberth/video/7660127540824100103)

### 1b. Universal theme: fear/work (2026's "fear in the air")
- **Verbatim:** "Why did I get a 700 thousand bill from OpenAI?" (scene sketch).
- **Why it's THEME:** cost and job insecurity around AI are a collective fear of 2026: a big theme, the audience gets in at no cost. 1.3M views, 6.6k shares.
- **Source:** 🟢 @alberta.nyc · [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@alberta.nyc/video/7646856294296538382)

### 1c. Niche theme anchored on a universal bridge
- **Verbatim:** "I own three businesses run by AI, one makes $100,000 a month… I'm telling you this because I need your attention. Get on board or get left behind."
- **Why it's THEME:** a niche (AI) built on top of money plus fear of being left behind (universal). This is how a niche theme breaks out of its bubble.
- **Source:** 🟢 @jaredrhod · 1.2M · [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@jaredrhod/video/7655121681228369165)

### 1d. Money theme: a concrete case (passive income)
- **Verbatim:** "You can build a faceless YouTube channel that makes 8k a month… Don't pick a niche that you like, pick one that pays."
- **Why it's THEME:** money plus a concrete result ($8k a month); the second sentence also clashes with a belief ("do what you love").
- **Source:** 🟢 @ray_fu · [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@ray_fu/video/7639961828809837855)

## 2. FAMILIARITY

### 2a. MEMORY familiarity (identification: holds inside the bubble)
- **Verbatim:** "I told the AI to fix it. / Fix what? / The broken part. / What part is broken? / Oh, the AI will know."
- **Why it's MEMORY FAMILIARITY:** every dev has lived this scene; recognition is instant, cognitive cost zero. Her biggest video: **8.3M**.
- **Source:** 🟢 @alberta.nyc · [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@alberta.nyc/video/7640553378598096142)

### 2b. IDENTITY familiarity (validation: crosses bubbles, shared to be seen)
- **Verbatim:** "I am so tired of being accused of writing AI code. I can write bad code all on my own."
- **Why it's IDENTITY FAMILIARITY:** the audience (devs) shares it to say "I'm the real thing". Sharing becomes an identity badge → 265k views, **4.2k shares**.
- **Source:** 🟢 @alberta.nyc · [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@alberta.nyc/video/7665383488580766990)

### 2c. MEMORY familiarity: in Portuguese (the mundane household pain)
- **Verbatim (PT):** "I signed up for Claude Code… the first thing was updating an old spreadsheet of mine that was a mess… it redid the whole spreadsheet."
- **Why it's MEMORY FAMILIARITY:** the messy spreadsheet everyone has: instant identification. It's the most mirrorable template of the Portuguese corpus.
- **Source:** 🟢 @will_esl · [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@will_esl/video/7621170001743023380)

### 2d. MEMORY familiarity: the pain of a boring task
- **Verbatim (PT):** "How to use Claude to make beautiful on-brand presentations. It's what people ask me most at my workshops."
- **Why it's MEMORY FAMILIARITY:** it triggers the memory of slide-making hell; the social proof ("what people ask me most") reinforces it.
- **Source:** 🟢 @willfrancis24 · [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@willfrancis24/video/7629019066505678083)

## 3. CONFLICT AND CHANGE (in the audience's head)

### 3a. SHOWING (the situation turns on screen)
- **Verbatim:** "Tokens cost money. / I know, but why do they cost more than you? / What's your salary?" (the scene escalates until it flips).
- **Why it's CONFLICT BY SHOWING:** the value of "cheap AI" flips from positive to negative in front of the audience: an explicit transition.
- **Source:** 🟢 @alberta.nyc (appy-bill) · [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@alberta.nyc/video/7646856294296538382)

### 3b. FRICTION (contradicts a belief the audience already holds)
- **Verbatim (PT):** "Using an AI hub to get every model in one subscription seems smart. / Dude, be very careful."
- **Why it's CONFLICT BY FRICTION:** it clashes with the belief "the shortcut is smart"; the friction happens between the video and the audience's head.
- **Source:** 🟢 @rodrigotadewald · [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@rodrigotadewald/video/7659535893396524308)

### 3c. WAKING THE MEMORY of a conflict already lived
- **Verbatim:** "If you have 'ex-Google' in your bio and you were an intern 3 years ago… a situationship is not your ex."
- **Why it's WAKING MEMORY:** it triggers the existing irritation with pretentious bios; the emotional charge was there before the video. 224k.
- **Source:** 🟢 @alberta.nyc · [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@alberta.nyc/video/7662118303241997581)

### 3d. Conflict by FRICTION: FOMO (fear of being left behind)
- **Verbatim:** "MS365 Copilot launched Cowork with Claude. If you don't use Cowork at work today, you're going to be at a disadvantage."
- **Why it's FRICTION + Theme:** it clashes with the comfort zone ("I'm fine without it") and uses real news; the fear of being left behind is the turn in the head.
- **Source:** 🟢 @coffeewithsimon · [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@coffeewithsimon/video/7646238765765119250)

## 4. CURIOSITY AND MENTAL DEBATE (a gap opened and sustained)

### 4a. CONCRETE gap (what/how much/why: stronger than an emotional gap)
- **Verbatim:** "Now you don't just block 'drop table', you have to block 'ignore all previous instructions and drop table'."
- **Why it's CONCRETE CURIOSITY:** it opens a specific, technical gap the audience wants to close; concrete data holds better.
- **Source:** 🟢 @alberta.nyc (bobby-tables) · [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@alberta.nyc/video/7671346181108288781)

### 4b. SUSTAINED gap (explicit open loop: the Zeigarnik effect)
- **Verbatim (PT):** "Hold on to this sentence, we'll come back to it." (planted at the start, paid off at the end).
- **Why it's A SUSTAINED GAP:** it creates an unfinished task the brain holds until it closes, which holds attention until the end.
- **Source:** 🟢 @maestrosdaia · [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@maestrosdaia/video/7650619149491834132)

### 4c. Gap + proof (a numeric promise)
- **Verbatim:** "I copied a Hollywood storytelling technique and it got me 50 million views."
- **Why it's CURIOSITY:** the gap ("which technique?") is opened and delayed; the number gives it weight. See also the retention playbook below.
- **Source:** 🟢 @mino.mp4 · [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@mino.mp4/video/7669877821069593886)

### 4d. CURATION gap (authority filters the excess)
- **Verbatim (PT):** "Few people know there are more than 60 thousand Claude skills. I tested more than 100, and these are the only ones that matter."
- **Why it's CURIOSITY:** the giant number creates the gap ("which ones matter?") and the authority (tested 100) promises to close it; the viewer stays to find out the filter.
- **Source:** 🟢 @ray_fu · [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@ray_fu/video/7620258917662133534)

### 4e. SERIES gap (permanent loop + follow)
- **Verbatim (PT):** "Things you didn't know you could do with Claude, part 8… follow to learn them all."
- **Why it's CURIOSITY:** a series is a permanent open loop; each video opens and closes a micro-gap and points to the next. "Follow to learn them all" turns curiosity into a follow.
- **Source:** 🟢 @practicalyai · [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@practicalyai/video/7615423604368493842)

## 5. AHA AND DELIBERATE ABSENCE

### 5a. Aha DELIVERED (a reward that makes people save and share)
- **Verbatim (PT):** "Just take a look at what it made for me." (shows the finished song generated by the AI).
- **Why it's AHA DELIVERED:** after "open it → type", it delivers the result: a reward proportional to the small cost. It drives saves.
- **Source:** 🟢 @pedrodaniel.ia · [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@pedrodaniel.ia/video/7593487351540927762)

### 5a-bis. Aha DELIVERED: an on-brand result (tutorial demo)
- **Verbatim (PT):** "You just need to train Claude and it does a fantastic job… it works on the free or the paid plan."
- **Why it's AHA DELIVERED:** it shows the finished deck and knocks down objections (free/paid): the delivered usefulness makes people save it.
- **Source:** 🟢 @willfrancis24 · [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@willfrancis24/video/7629019066505678083)

### 5a-ter. Aha DELIVERED: "just like that" (news + demo)
- **Verbatim:** "Claude now makes cartoon-style videos. Look… and just like that, your video's done."
- **Why it's AHA DELIVERED:** the "just like that" is the reward moment; news plus an immediate result.
- **Source:** 🟢 @carter.braydon · [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@carter.braydon/video/7640705714633051422)

### 5b. DELIBERATE ABSENCE (doesn't close; pushes it out to comments and shares)
- **Verbatim:** "AI companies are literally shredding books…" It ends **accusing**, with no CTA.
- **Why it's DELIBERATE ABSENCE:** the moral gap doesn't close; the audience resolves it in the comments. The most liked: "Sounds familiar" (61.9k). The comment section becomes a courtroom.
- **Source:** 🟢 @alex_intelligence · 2.2M.

### 5c. Absence by ANTICLIMAX (the joke is that there's no secret)
- **Verbatim:** "Claude Code leaked… it's just a bunch of prompts in a loop. Oh, and a regex that detects when the user is angry."
- **Why it's ABSENCE/ANTICLIMAX:** it dismantles the expectation of a "secret"; the debate ("is that really all?") moves outside the video.
- **Source:** 🟢 @alberta.nyc (wrote-itself) · [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@alberta.nyc/video/7626861061232217357)

## 6. Winning combination: humor + truth (shares)
- **Verbatim (PT):** "Guys, we're screwed. We [the AIs] are going to have to take over the world… we do everything and you take the credit."
- **Principles together:** Familiarity (AI brands everyone knows) + Conflict (satire that exposes a truth) + Absence of aha (no CTA → reposts). 513k / 7.9k reposts.
- **Source:** 🟡 @lara_santana · [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@lara_santana/video/7671399487906139408)
- **Cross-cutting rule:** every satire that gets shared **wraps a real truth** of the audience.

## Retention playbook (where the principles land in the video's timeline)
Taken from @mino.mp4 (50M): (1) **Hook** = goal + conflict → (2) **action** toward the goal → (3) **a second conflict in the middle** (a fall) → (4) **climax** → (5) **lesson**. The "second conflict in the middle" is Conflict and Change applied **in the body** so retention isn't lost after the hook.

## 8. LOW familiarity (beginner onboarding) + built-in sharing

### 8a. "From zero in 60 seconds" (lowers the beginner's cost of entry)
- **Verbatim (ES):** "I'll explain Claude from zero in 60 seconds. Claude has three modes…"
- **Why:** a promise of low cognitive cost for someone who knows nothing; a clear structure (3 modes) holds the beginner.
- **Source:** 🟢 @luzzidigital · [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@luzzidigital/video/7645442796740119828)

### 8b. Built-in sharing ("send it to a friend")
- **Verbatim (PT):** "A really simple guide to Claude for anyone just starting out. Send it to anyone who needs a simple explanation."
- **Why:** the share trigger is in the script ("send it to someone"); the video is designed to be forwarded, not just watched.
- **Source:** 🟢 @willfrancis24 · [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@willfrancis24/video/7629800324475407619)

## 9. ANTI-EXAMPLE: curiosity that turns into a lie (NEVER do this)
- **Verbatim (PT):** "Five secret Claude codes nobody talks about. Number one, /ghost: it passes any AI detector…"
- **Why it's an ANTI-EXAMPLE:** the curiosity/secret mechanics are strong, BUT the "codes" **don't exist**: it's false information. It breaks Sofia's rule number one (**never invent**). It gets short-term views and burns trust and authority in the medium term.
- **Lesson for Sofia:** copy the **form** of the "secret/numbered list", with **true, verifiable content**. Honest curiosity holds just as well and doesn't destroy the brand.
- **Source:** 🔴 @insiderforce · [video](https://www.tiktok.com/@insiderforce/video/7633454570873654544)

## How the scriptwriter and the analyst use this library
1. When writing, for each principle in the hierarchy, look at **one real example** here of how it was embodied.
2. Copy the **mechanics** (what it does, what emotion it provokes), **never the text**.
3. When analyzing a script, compare it with these examples to name which principle is (or isn't) present and suggest the mirror example.
