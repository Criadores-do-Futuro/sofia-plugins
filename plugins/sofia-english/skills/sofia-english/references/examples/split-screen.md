# Analyzed examples: Split screen

The quoted script lines below are translated from the Portuguese original (it narrates a scene from the Brazilian edition of MasterChef). Learn the shape, never copy the lines.

## Example 1: MasterChef (arrogance or confidence?)

**Format:** Split screen: a person narrating a scene from a TV show (see [split screen](../formats/split-screen.md))
**Structure:** Rising conflict → Turn → Moral

---

> "Look where arrogance can take you."

(Curiosity) "What did he do?" "Did he blow it?" Two loops in one sentence.

> "This guy said he was gonna make, like, on the MasterChef show, he said: 'I'm going to make the perfect chicken.'"

(Familiarity + Curiosity) MasterChef is well known. "Perfect chicken" sounds arrogant: you want to see if he nails it or flops.

> "And the host goes: 'Perfect chicken? Why is it perfect?'"

(Curiosity intensifies) She asks the question the audience was already asking. Retention.

> "He says: 'I've made it over 600 times.'"

(Mental debate) "600 times" surprises. The viewer thinks "no way" or "who counts that?"

> "She doubts it, right? Who counts how many times they've made chicken?"

(Identification) The narrator says what the audience thought. "Right?" is conversation, not a script.

> "Then he says: 'It's my pre-workout, I make this chicken about four times a week.'"

His defense starts to build. It's logical: not empty arrogance.

> "Then she says: 'Oh, so you've been doing it about two years.'"

(Conflict) She tries to catch him. Tension rises.

> "Then he answers: 'No, not two, three. Three years. I counted last night.'"

(Contrast) He turns the tables. He came prepared. "I counted last night" = a detail that makes it feel real.

> "Then she keeps pushing: 'But you know perfect is an opinion, right?'"

(Conflict: progression) The pressure continues. Identification: everyone has had someone doubt what they can do.

> "And they keep at it: '600 times?' 'Perfect chicken?'"

(Progression) Tension at its peak.

> "And she finally tastes the chicken…"

(Curiosity: maximum) Suspense. Did it work or not?

> "And she comes over and says: 'Fábio, the way you cooked this chicken... It's perfect. I'd eat this dish 600 times.'"

(Aha + Contrast) THE TURN. Fear → relief. Doubt → confirmation. "600 times" comes back: she validates him using his own number.

> "So, in your life, don't confuse arrogance with confidence."

(Moral) One sentence. It leaves the story and comes into the audience's life.

> "If you don't believe in yourself, who else will?"

(Familiar language) A line everyone has heard. After this story, it hits differently.

> "Nobody can be bigger than the image they have of themselves."

(Aha) The lesson the viewer takes home.

> "If deep down you feel the urge to have a bigger image of yourself... tap the link in my bio."

A natural CTA. "Deep down" speaks to whoever identified. It doesn't feel like an ad.

---

### Patterns to learn
1. "Then he says," "then she says": narrating like you're telling a friend
2. Progressive conflict: each line climbs, never drops
3. The narrator says what the audience thinks ("who counts how many times they made chicken?")
4. The turn comes after maximum tension: the more pressure, the stronger the relief
5. The moral in ONE sentence
6. The CTA as a consequence of identification

### What today's method would change
In a new script, the narrator's rhetorical questions ("She doubts it, right?", "who else will?") become statements unless they come from a real comment; see [the Floor](../floor.md).
