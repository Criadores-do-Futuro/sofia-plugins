# Analyzed examples: Visual storytelling

The quoted script lines below are translated from the Portuguese original. The people named are Brazilian influencers named by the original creator; the claims about them are hers. Learn the shape, never copy the lines.

## Example 1: The crash (Gato Preto and Bia Miranda)

**Format:** Visual storytelling: images illustrate what she says (see [visual storytelling](../formats/visual-storytelling.md))
**Structure:** Rising emotional ladder → Reveal → Moral with contrast

---

> "Picture this scene."

(Curiosity: visual) A crash image appears while she talks. The car-crash effect: everybody stops to look.

> "Your dad, an older man, got up early even though he was tired, just to drive you to college."

(Identification + Emotional relevance) "Your dad" puts you inside the scene. A situation many people have lived.

> "He drives calmly, watches the lights, follows the rules of the road."

(Familiar language) Action verbs create a mental image. It builds a calm that is about to be destroyed.

> "And suddenly, a car runs the red light at high speed, coming home from a club after a whole night of drinking."

(Contrast: strong) Calm → chaos. Specific details (red light, club, whole night) make it feel real. Anger and outrage.

> "Inside that car is a couple laughing, smoking, filming stories, living like there are no consequences in this life."

(Progression) Three actions in a row paint the picture. Each detail raises the anger.

> "And that car hits yours, hurts you, puts your dad's life at risk."

(Emotional relevance) Back to YOU. Your dad at risk. Fear + anger.

> "And worst of all, the driver still has the nerve to get out yelling and getting in his face, ready to go after your dad."

(Progression + Familiar language) "And worst of all" climbs one more step. "Go after" is conversational language.

> "Now stop and think: these are the people who get applause, views, deals and tons of fame here on social media."

(Contrast + Curiosity) Leaves the scene for the real world. The reckless one is famous. "Who is she talking about?"

> "An underworld that maybe you, who are in my bubble, have never bumped into, but that's gigantic and moves millions."

(Identification) "My bubble" creates a group. You and she are on the same side.

> "You've maybe never heard the names I'm about to say: Gato Preto and Bia Miranda."

(Aha) Closes the loop. Now you know who she's talking about.

> "But they're role models for thousands who watch and pay for their lifestyle."

(Contrast) You don't know them BUT thousands pay for it.

> "The truth is, the internet built a stage where whoever screams, whoever shocks the most, whoever shows off the most... grows the most. And the worse, the better."

(Moral) A rhythmic line. "And the worse, the better" closes with impact.

> "Meanwhile, the life of someone who supports a family, pays the bills, studies and tries to do better is invisible."

(Contrast) The viewer sees themselves. "Invisible" is the word that hurts.

---

### Patterns to learn
1. "Your dad" instead of "a dad": puts the viewer INSIDE the scene
2. An emotional ladder that only climbs, never drops
3. Three details in a row paint the picture
4. "And worst of all" signals the next step up
5. A final word that hurts ("invisible")

### What today's method would change
In a new script, "The truth is" gets cut (throat-clearing) and the word "bubble" stays out of the spoken script (the audience doesn't need to know a bubble exists). See [no AI-speak](../no-ai-speak.md) and [breaking into a bubble](../breaking-the-bubble.md).
