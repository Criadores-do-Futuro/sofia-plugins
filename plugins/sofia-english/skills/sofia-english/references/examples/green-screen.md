# Analyzed examples: Green screen

The quoted script lines below are translated from the Portuguese originals; links, names and numbers are the original creators' claims, unchanged. Check every fact and number in a primary source before reusing it (see [facts and sources](../facts-and-sources.md)). Learn the shape, never copy the lines.

## Example 1: Lady Gaga (bullied in college)

**Link:** https://www.instagram.com/reel/DHrok4KtbrE/
**Format:** Green screen: background images illustrating the story (see [green screen](../formats/green-screen.md))

---

[Universal conflict loaded with emotional relevance and familiarity: bullying, everyone has been through it or seen it. Triggers anger, empathy, outrage]
This girl got mocked so much in college that students made a Facebook group called "Stephanie Germanotta, you will never be famous."

[Opens with contrast: a student BUT dreaming of becoming a great artist]
Stephanie was a freshman music student at New York University, and her dream had always been to become a great artist.

She was always performing at all kinds of venues to get experience.

[Conflict: always criticized]
But some of her classmates criticized her a lot.

[Familiar language and identification: many people have heard lines like these]
Comments like: "Who does she think she is?" "She's always trying to show off," and they laughed at her behind her back.

[Raises emotional relevance. Familiar language: you picture the scene. Raises the conflict]
Some people even stepped on her show flyers with dirty boots before putting them back up.

[Familiar language: "hate," Facebook groups. Raises the conflict]
But worst of all was a group of 12 students who created a Facebook just to hate on Stephanie and say she'd never achieve her dreams.

Even with all that, this 18-year-old never gave up.

[Climax: a big change. Her life was never the same]
Not long after, she dropped out of college to chase her dream on her own.

[Familiarity: Lady Gaga is known worldwide. Aha: you find out the girl is Lady Gaga]
And today, Lady Gaga has become one of the biggest superstars in the world.

Many years later, she talked about that Facebook group, saying:

[Moral: don't give up when people doubt you]
"That's why you can't give up when people doubt you."

## Example 2: Coke Zero (the "Celebrity Nutritionist")

**Link:** https://www.instagram.com/p/DIgRFfPuJ7j/

---

[Mental debate: "is it poison?" "is it safe?" Opens a curiosity loop]
Coke Zero has 36 milligrams of aspartame,

[Conflict: coma]
which our body breaks down into methanol, which can put you in a coma.

[Raises the conflict: a threat to the brain. Emotional relevance: fear of cognitive damage]
Phenylalanine, which can damage the brain.

[Raises it with "cancer"]
And aspartic acid, which causes cancer.

[Contrast: Coke has methanol BUT a tomato has more. Mental debate. Familiarity: everyone eats tomatoes]
But what nobody told you is that a tomato has 6 times more methanol than a Coke Zero.

[Contrast: phenylalanine BUT steak has more. Familiarity: everyone eats steak]
A steak has 32 times more phenylalanine than a Coke Zero.

[Contrast: aspartic acid BUT an egg has more. Familiarity: everyone eats eggs]
And an egg has 34 times more aspartic acid than a Coke Zero.

[Familiar language: the saying "the dose makes the poison." Aha: 50 cans to do harm]
Literally anything can be bad for your health, it depends on the dose. But for aspartame, studies show a safe dose of up to 50 milligrams per kilogram of body weight. So for a regular person, it would take 50 cans of Coke for aspartame to start being a problem.

[Aha: water overdose reinforces the idea]
But before that happened, you'd have to drink those 16 liters of water, which in this case would give you a water overdose, also known as hyponatremia.

[Change: the audience changes how it sees Coke Zero]
Swapping regular Coke for Coke Zero is the easiest thing you can do to lose fat.

[Moral + CTA. Familiarity: you think of someone who trashes Coke]
Next time someone tells you Coke Zero is going to kill you, send them this video, because people who follow the Celebrity Nutritionist learn nutrition in a fun, illustrated way.

---

## Coke Zero diagnosis

1. **Idea:** "Coke Zero isn't harmful: the dose makes the poison"
2. **Theme:** Health and food, universal (everyone eats and drinks)
3. **Conflict:** In the audience's head (contradicts the belief that Coke = poison)
4. **Gaps:** "So is Coke poison?" (held) → closes with the safe dose
5. **Aha:** Several mini-ahas (tomato > Coke, steak > Coke) + a final aha (50 cans)

The diagnosis follows the order in [diagnosis](../diagnosis.md) and [principles](../principles.md).

### What today's method would change
"What nobody told you" is throat-clearing, and "literally" is a filler adverb: both get cut in a new script (see [no AI-speak](../no-ai-speak.md)). Health claims like these need a primary source opened in the session before they enter a script.
