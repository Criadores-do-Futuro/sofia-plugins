# Them, Me, You, Future structure

**Them → Me → You → Future**

## Overview

Turns a personal experience or an observation into a shared narrative. It starts outside the speaker and ends inside the viewer. Four simple turns, and strong ones.

## Steps

### 1. Them

Someone else's story: a friend, a creator you admire, a famous figure, a case that went viral. It creates curiosity and shows the idea is bigger than you. The person and the story must be real and checkable.

**Example** (shape only; use a real case with a source): "I met a creator who gained {number with source} followers in {time}. But it wasn't just the algorithm..."

### 2. Me

How it hit you, taught you or challenged you. This is the moment of vulnerability or of taking a position:

- "When I saw that, I thought about my own path."
- "It reminded me of something I went through."
- "I've been through something like that too."

**Example:** "Around the same time, I was stuck. Watching him grow made me question everything I was doing wrong."

No fabricated personal experience: the "Me" comes from what the creator actually lived.

### 3. You

Bring the audience inside. The reflection becomes an invitation ("maybe you feel like you're banging your head against a wall too"). A challenge stated as a fact works too: "Maybe what's holding you back is the way you tell your story, and not a lack of talent."

This is where the audience recognizes itself or moves.

### 4. Future

What could happen if the viewer applies, or doesn't apply, what you shared. They become the main character: you plant an idea of a possible future, never a promised result.

**Example:** "If you stop creating to please the algorithm and start creating with structure, your next {milestone} might be closer than you think."

## Why it works

- It makes room to tell stories without sounding like you're showing off
- The audience steps into the message more naturally
- It builds an emotional climb plus logic plus direction
- It closes with clarity, movement and purpose

## Golden tip

Before writing, answer: what message do I want to leave, and what action or reflection do I want to spark? Without that, the structure doesn't close: it only really works when you know what you're planting at the end.

## Quick checklist

- **ST.them-me-you-future.1.** Does "Them" create curiosity about someone real?
- **ST.them-me-you-future.2.** Does "Me" show genuine vulnerability or a genuine position?
- **ST.them-me-you-future.3.** Does "You" bring the audience into the story?
- **ST.them-me-you-future.4.** Does "Future" project something the audience wants to reach?
- **ST.them-me-you-future.5.** Was the final message clear before writing started?
