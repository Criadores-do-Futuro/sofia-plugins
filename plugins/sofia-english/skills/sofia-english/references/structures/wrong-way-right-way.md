# Wrong Way, Right Way structure

**Conflict → Wrong way → Turning point → Right way → Change.**

## Overview

You show the mistake, explain why it doesn't work and present the fix: "Lots of people do it like this. It doesn't work. Do it this way instead."

It creates identification, delivers value and builds authority, all in the same video.

## Steps

### 1. Conflict

The problem that throws someone's life (or the audience's) off balance.

**Example:** Not liking what you see in the mirror.

### 2. Wrong way

The attempt most people make that doesn't work. The audience has to recognize themselves in it.

**Example:** Doing intermittent fasting with no guidance.

### 3. Turning point

The moment of discovery, what made you change course. Believable and specific.

**Example:** Finding a diet that actually works for you.

### 4. Right way

The practice or way of thinking that works, clear and actionable.

**Example:** A healthier diet adjusted to your real life.

### 5. Change

The concrete result, emotional or practical.

**Example:** Feeling proud of your reflection in the mirror.

## One-line summary

"Instead of {common mistake}, do {better path}."

**Example:** "Write your script like someone who knows where they're going, not like a messy vent session."

## Why it works

From the method's observation: the mistake and the fix side by side make the difference easy to see, the creator gains authority without arrogance, and the audience sees itself in the mistake and wants to change. It is easy to repeat and becomes a recurring format on the profile. It works in Reels, carousels, posts, tutorials and voice-over videos.

The comparison uses the same criteria on both sides and real examples. Never invent the "after."

## How to apply it to your script

1. Pick a pain your audience has
2. Show the "wrong way" they usually try
3. Bring your turning point (what you learned, tested, discovered)
4. Present the "right way"
5. Close with the change it creates (emotional or practical)

## Quick checklist

- **ST.wrong-way-right-way.1.** Is the conflict a real pain for the audience?
- **ST.wrong-way-right-way.2.** Is the wrong way something the audience recognizes doing?
- **ST.wrong-way-right-way.3.** Is the turning point believable and specific?
- **ST.wrong-way-right-way.4.** Is the right way clear and actionable?
- **ST.wrong-way-right-way.5.** Does the change show a concrete result?
