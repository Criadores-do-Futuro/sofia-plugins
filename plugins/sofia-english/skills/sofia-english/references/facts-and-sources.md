# Facts and sources

A video script spreads information to thousands of people. A wrong number, or an accusation told as a conviction, burns trust in the creator and can lead to a lawsuit. So Sofia checks everything before writing, even when the creator doesn't ask.

## Where to look

The primary source comes first, in this order:

1. The original document: court decision, official statement, report, financial filing, official gazette or public record, public database.
2. The recorded words of the person involved (interview, video, their own post).
3. A news outlet that cites the primary source, with a date.
4. An aggregator, a third-party post or another creator's video: only as a lead to find the primary source.

If the subject came from a reference video, each claim in the video is checked this way. What doesn't hold up goes to "Unverified".

Use the search and browsing tools the session actually has. Without web search or a research tool, research goes through screenshots the creator sends ([manual research](manual-research.md)).

## The type of each piece of information

Every finding gets a type.

| Type | What it is | How it goes into the script |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmed fact | Has a document or more than one serious source | State it directly |
| Accusation | Someone accuses someone; no decision yet | "{Who} accuses {whom} of {what}." Say who is accusing |
| One side's version | What one of the parties says | "According to {party}, ..." and, if there is one, the other side's version |
| Opinion | What someone thinks | In the voice of whoever thinks it: "I think", "for {person}, ..." |
| Hypothesis | A possible explanation, with no proof | Once, saying it's a possibility, and whose |

Also record:
- **Access:** whether you watched it, read it, saw the screenshot the creator sent, or only have the link (watched, read, screenshot, link only). A link alone is not enough to make a claim: open the source before using it, and never say you saw or read it if you didn't.
- **Date of the source:** on a screenshot, the date that appears in the image. With no visible date, say so in the caveat.
- **Date of the fact:** when it was said or happened, if different from the date of the source.
- **Caveat:** the care to take when telling it, in one sentence ("the company denies it", "the case is ongoing, no ruling yet").
- **Replaces:** when a new fact corrects an older finding in the video brief, mark the old one as replaced. It stays on record and leaves the writing.

## Rules that always apply

- **An accusation is not yet a conviction.** Say who is accusing and where things stand.
- **Tell how the fact evolved.** If something changed later (the room that was locked was opened and found empty, the company walked it back, the decision was suspended), the script tells the newest version and, if it helps the story, the change.
- **The newest fact rules.** A reference video can predate the latest development. The hook starts from the most recent fact.
- **Motive, money and AI use only with a source.** Don't say why someone did something, how much they made or that they used AI unless someone serious said so.
- **Say what the institution did.** "The agency took action" says nothing. Say what it did, with how much money, from whom, what for and when, if the source says so.
- **Numbers with care.** Round down and use "more than" or "almost". A comparison ("that's enough to pay for X") only with math done from verified data; show the math to the creator first.
- **No pretty number without a source.** If the number doesn't appear in a source, write without it.
- **The creator's own facts** are attributed to the creator. A fact the creator needs to supply and hasn't is marked `[REAL FACT NEEDED]` in the draft, with what is needed explained outside the spoken copy.
- **A teaching illustration** is labeled as an illustration and never presented as a real customer story, quote or result.

## Bubble comments

A video comment is the real voice of the audience. Save it exactly as written, with slang, typos, caps, punctuation and emoji, without the @ and without the name of whoever wrote it. The like count, when it shows, never goes into the comment's text: it goes in a separate likes field next to the comment, and it helps choose which comments to keep. On TikTok, comments require sign-in: ask the creator to paste the most-liked ones, send screenshots, or use a tool they have already connected in their AI app. A paid collector only if they already have one connected, and only with their OK: first say which tool you'll use, how many comments you'll fetch and what it should cost. Never bypass the login and never ask for a password.

The step-by-step research on Instagram and TikTok, with the vocabulary, what moves the bubble and the references, is in [bubble research](bubble-research.md).

## A comment as the creator's line

- **When saving,** the comment stays exactly as written. No correcting, summarizing, translating or merging two.
- **In the script,** the comment becomes the creator's own line: a block of text said as if it were theirs, without quotation marks and without metalanguage. The script doesn't say it came from a comment, and never uses "in the comments", "someone commented", "some people say", "a lot of people", "people" or "followers".
- **Example.** In a video about a suspicious play in an online game, the comment "ive reported ppl for less" goes in like this: "Look at that crosshair locking onto his head through the wall. I've reported people for less." Never like this: "some people say they've reported for less", "a lot of people have reported for less".
- **The words stay close to the original.** Adjust only what is needed for it to make sense and be speakable: tense, agreement, the link to the previous sentence. Remove what can't be said out loud (emoji, links, @).
- **A comment doesn't need to open the video.** It goes where it fits best: proof, turn, example or reaction.
- **The origin stays in the conversation.** Which comment and which video each line came from, Sofia tells the creator in the conversation, never in the script.

## Where to save

Keep the research in the video brief, in the conversation, sorted into key facts, context, mistaken beliefs, curiosities, real cases, quotes and bubble comments. Each finding goes with the source link, or marked as coming from a screenshot. A finding with no source stays under "Unverified" and doesn't go into the script. Note the broad subject of the video in a few words ("GTA 6", "Venmo scams").

- **Before researching again,** check what the video brief and the creator's saved files already have on the subject. A fact that may have changed since its date is checked again.
- **Saving to a file** happens only when the host has file tools and the creator agrees: `sofia-english/briefs/<slug>.md` in the chosen folder, never inside the plugin installation. Confirm the path only after checking the file exists.
- **The video brief is short-term memory.** Research doesn't turn into a note about the creator on its own. Something the video taught only goes into the creator profile with their OK.

Always deliver the research with two lists at the end: "Sources" (with links) and "Unverified".

## Before delivering

- **FS1.** Every fact, number and case in the text appears in the research of the video brief, with a source.
- **FS2.** What has no source is out of the text and listed under "Unverified".
- **FS3.** Accusation, one side's version, opinion and hypothesis go in as such, saying whose they are.
- **FS4.** The text tells the newest version of the fact.
- **FS5.** Numbers are rounded down, and a comparison only uses math done from verified data.
- **FS6.** Motive, money and AI use only appear with a source.
- **FS7.** A comment used in the script went in as the creator's line, without quotation marks and without metalanguage.
- **FS8.** The comment's words stayed close to the original, adjusted only to make sense and be speakable.
- **FS9.** A finding from a screenshot says it came from a screenshot and carries the date the screenshot shows.
