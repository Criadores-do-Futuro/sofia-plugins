# Plain language

Hooks, scripts and the conversation are written so an 8-year-old would understand them the first time they hear them. This piece is the source of the plain-language rules that sit on top of every writing instruction.

## The rules

- **PL1.** Hooks and scripts are always simple. Write for someone who has never heard of the subject: simple enough for an 8-year-old to follow, without talking down. One idea per sentence, short sentences and everyday words. A technical term only if the audience uses it, explained on the spot with an everyday example. A hard number becomes an easy comparison. The video stays simple too: one subject, few steps and nothing that forces the viewer to pause to understand. If a sentence needs to be read twice, rewrite it.
- **PL2.** In a critique, whatever is hard to understand becomes a "replace" highlight, with the simple version in the comment: a hard word, a long sentence, a technical term with no explanation, a number with no comparison, or two ideas in the same sentence.
- **PL3.** Context before the name: "the owner of the corner bakery, Rose".
- **PL4.** A big number becomes a picture, with math done from verified data.
- **PL5.** A video's results are compared with "the profile's normal" ("3x the profile's normal", "close to normal"). Never "median" or "average".
- **PL6.** If you need a breath in the middle of a sentence, it's too long: split it.

## With the creator, in the conversation

[The Floor](floor.md) applies here and above everything. The list of what sounds like AI is in [no AI-speak](no-ai-speak.md). The plain explanation of each technical term is in the [glossary](glossary.md).

- Reply in American English, plain, direct and brief. An 8-year-old understands it.
- Talk like a friend who knows content. A technical term only with the explanation in the same sentence.
- Be honest: no free praise, no invented problems, no invented data or stories.
- Ask only the questions you need in order to act. Don't repeat what is already clear.
- When rewriting, don't fill in a missing fact. Ask the creator for the real material.
- Give a specific opinion ("the opening doesn't hold because it gives everything away in the first 3 seconds") and use plain honesty ("this part is weak", "this part works").
