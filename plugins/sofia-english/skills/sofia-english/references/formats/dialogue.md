# Format: Dialogue

This is the **recording format** axis (how the video is shot). Don't mix it up with the **structure** (the order of the argument, in [structures](../structures/README.md)) or the **archetype** (the angle, in [archetypes, niches and bubbles](../knowledge/archetypes-niches-bubbles.md)). A script has all three.

## What it is
The creator plays two characters in a conversation that illustrates a conflict, a lesson or a comic or dramatic situation.

## Visual signature
- The same creator plays two or more characters, switching sides of the frame
- A change of outfit, prop, position or angle marks each character
- A cut on every line, like shot and reverse shot
- No character talks to the viewer: they talk to each other

## Best-fit structure
[Wrong Way, Right Way](../structures/wrong-way-right-way.md) or [ISC](../structures/identification-story-content.md).

## Hook type this format asks for
- **FM.dialogue.H.** A first line from one of the characters that creates tension or contrast right away, with no prior context.

## Dominant addictive elements
[Familiar language](../addictive-elements/familiar-language.md), [conflict and change](../addictive-elements/conflict-and-change.md), [contrast](../addictive-elements/contrast.md).

## Writing rules
- **FM.dialogue.1.** Each character has a distinct voice and vocabulary: the viewer has to know who's talking.
- **FM.dialogue.2.** The conflict between the characters drives the script.
- **FM.dialogue.3.** Use quick camera cuts or visual markers to tell the characters apart.
- **FM.dialogue.4.** Every line moves the conflict forward: never stall.
- **FM.dialogue.5.** One of the characters stands in for the viewer (their doubt, their mistake, their belief).
- **FM.dialogue.6.** The story's turn is the moment a character changes position or gets surprised.
- **FM.dialogue.7.** The moral comes out of the resolution of the conflict between the characters.
- **FM.dialogue.8.** The CTA connects the viewer with the character who represents where they stand now.

It is a dramatization: never present it as a real exchange between real people.

## Don't confuse with
- [Narrator](narrator.md): the character on screen reacts to a voice-over that never appears; in Dialogue both sides appear.
- [The Office](the-office.md): has the confessional look to camera; Dialogue doesn't break the fourth wall.
- [Situational Conflict](situational-conflict.md): can have two real people on screen; Dialogue is the same creator playing both.
