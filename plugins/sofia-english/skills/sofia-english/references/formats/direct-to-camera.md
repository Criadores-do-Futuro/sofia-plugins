# Format: Direct to Camera

This is the **recording format** axis (how the video is shot). Don't mix it up with the **structure** (the order of the argument, in [structures](../structures/README.md)) or the **archetype** (the angle, in [archetypes, niches and bubbles](../knowledge/archetypes-niches-bubbles.md)). A script has all three.

## What it is
The creator talks straight to the camera, like a pocket-size talk. No outside visual elements: just the creator, the voice and the argument.

## Visual signature
- The creator talking straight to camera, looking into the lens, in a fixed or nearly fixed shot
- No visual support carrying the content: no screenshot, no chart, no meaningful B-roll
- Cuts only to remove pauses (jump cuts), keeping the same framing
- On-screen captions are allowed: they repeat the spoken line and don't add content

## Best-fit structure
[ISC](../structures/identification-story-content.md) or [Wrong Way, Right Way](../structures/wrong-way-right-way.md).

## Hook type this format asks for
- **FM.direct-to-camera.H.** A direct provocation or a statement of consequence that challenges a belief the viewer holds.

## Dominant addictive elements
[Emotional relevance](../addictive-elements/emotional-relevance.md), [familiar language](../addictive-elements/familiar-language.md), [theme and moral](../addictive-elements/theme-and-moral.md).

## Writing rules
- **FM.direct-to-camera.1.** The hook challenges a belief or behavior the viewer has.
- **FM.direct-to-camera.2.** No more than 3 arguments per video: pick the strongest.
- **FM.direct-to-camera.3.** Each argument is short, direct and verifiable.
- **FM.direct-to-camera.4.** Use concrete examples: never argue in the abstract. The argument has to work without visual assets the creator doesn't have.
- **FM.direct-to-camera.5.** The language sounds like the creator talking to a friend, not presenting to an audience.
- **FM.direct-to-camera.6.** Build toward the moral like a discovery, not a preached conclusion.
- **FM.direct-to-camera.7.** The aha is the most powerful line in the video: save it for the end.
- **FM.direct-to-camera.8.** The CTA is a reflective question or an invitation to act right away.

## Don't confuse with
- [Off-Camera Conversation](off-camera-conversation.md): the gaze goes off the lens, as if talking to someone nearby; Direct to Camera looks into the lens.
- [Green Screen](green-screen.md): if a screenshot or chart behind the creator carries the argument, it's Green Screen.
- [Lo-Fi](lo-fi.md): is about deliberately raw production; Direct to Camera can be raw or highly produced.
