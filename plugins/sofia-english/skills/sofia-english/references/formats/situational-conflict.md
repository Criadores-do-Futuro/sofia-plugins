# Format: Situational Conflict

## What it is
The video opens on a strong conflict that looks real: an argument, a threat, an accident, a theft, a confrontation or an emergency. The conflict is the way in; afterward, the video can reveal the staging and move into an explanation, an analogy or a conversation.

## Visual signature
- Action or tension in the first seconds
- Characters with incompatible goals
- A concrete situation, understandable before any explanation
- A clear shift from the conflict to the main message

## Best-fit structure
[ISC](../structures/identification-story-content.md): visual impact, story/conflict, then conclusion/reveal.

## Hook type this format asks for
- **FM.situational-conflict.H.** An immersive scene inside the action: the first image and the first line are already in the middle of the conflict.

## Dominant addictive elements
[Conflict and change](../addictive-elements/conflict-and-change.md), [curiosity and aha](../addictive-elements/curiosity-and-aha.md), [emotional relevance](../addictive-elements/emotional-relevance.md).

## Writing rules
- **FM.situational-conflict.1.** Start inside the action; don't explain the scene before it happens.
- **FM.situational-conflict.2.** Give each character one simple, opposing goal.
- **FM.situational-conflict.3.** Use short, reactive, natural lines; explained conflict sounds fake.
- **FM.situational-conflict.4.** Plant an immediate question: "what happened?" or "how is this going to end?".
- **FM.situational-conflict.5.** Move to the message without breaking the opening's promise.
- **FM.situational-conflict.6.** The conclusion gives the scene a new meaning; it doesn't just drop it. Resolve the same conflict you opened.
- **FM.situational-conflict.7.** Avoid staging that could cause real risk or mislead third parties outside the video's context. Never present staged danger as real: the video reveals or makes clear that it was staged.

## Don't confuse with
- [Dialogue](dialogue.md): can have disagreement, but doesn't need an urgent situation that looks real.
- [Social Experiment](social-experiment.md): records real reactions from strangers; here the conflict can be staged.
- An aggressive verbal hook to camera: a conflict of ideas, not a situational conflict.
