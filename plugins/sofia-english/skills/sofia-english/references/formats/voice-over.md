# Format: Voice-Over

This is the **recording format** axis (how the video is shot). Don't mix it up with the **structure** (the order of the argument, in [structures](../structures/README.md)) or the **archetype** (the angle, in [archetypes, niches and bubbles](../knowledge/archetypes-niches-bubbles.md)). A script has all three.

## What it is
The creator narrates off camera while images, clips or text appear on screen. The script is built to be read as narration, not as direct talk to camera.

## Visual signature
- Voice-over from start to finish; the creator barely appears talking to camera
- Images, clips, screenshots or text following one another over the narration
- The cut follows the narration: each sentence changes the image
- Captions usually follow the voice

## Best-fit structure
[ISC](../structures/identification-story-content.md) or [Analysis](../structures/analysis.md).

## Hook type this format asks for
- **FM.voice-over.H.** An immersive scene or a statement of consequence that creates a strong mental image in the first sentence.

## Dominant addictive elements
[Curiosity and aha](../addictive-elements/curiosity-and-aha.md), [contrast](../addictive-elements/contrast.md), [conflict and change](../addictive-elements/conflict-and-change.md).

## Writing rules
- **FM.voice-over.1.** Every sentence creates a mental image: write for the viewer's eyes. Match each claim to relevant visual evidence, and list the footage still needed.
- **FM.voice-over.2.** Use short, rhythmic sentences: the narration needs room to breathe between them.
- **FM.voice-over.3.** Build the story like a miniature documentary.
- **FM.voice-over.4.** The progression is visual: where we are → conflict → turn → resolution.
- **FM.voice-over.5.** Avoid abstractions: everything must translate into an image.
- **FM.voice-over.6.** Use the present tense for immersion ("he walks in", not "he walked in").
- **FM.voice-over.7.** Deliver the aha at the exact moment the visual confirms the argument.
- **FM.voice-over.8.** Say the CTA as the inevitable consequence of what was just shown.

## Don't confuse with
- [Narrator](narrator.md): a character on screen REACTS to the off-screen voice; in Voice-Over the voice leads and the images illustrate.
- [Visual Storytelling](visual-storytelling.md): the story is told mainly through animated text and graphics; in Voice-Over the voice tells it.
- [Analysis](analysis.md): uses inserts as evidence, with the creator on screen interpreting; if the voice stays off camera from start to finish, it's Voice-Over.
