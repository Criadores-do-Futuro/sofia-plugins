# Format: Analogy

## What it is
A physical situation, object, everyday scene or fable makes an abstract idea visible. The path is concrete → explicit connection → new understanding. The payoff is the moment the two things click together.

## Visual signature
- A concrete object or situation plays the central role
- The concrete action moves forward before the explanation
- An explicit spoken connection to the abstract idea

## Best-fit structure
[ISC](../structures/identification-story-content.md), with the reveal in the close.

## Hook type this format asks for
- **FM.analogy.H.** An immersive scene with the concrete object in action, holding back the meaning. Or an unexpected contrast between the object and the topic ("This glass explains why you can't save money.").

## Dominant addictive elements
[Familiar language and mental images](../addictive-elements/familiar-language.md), [curiosity and aha](../addictive-elements/curiosity-and-aha.md), [theme and moral](../addictive-elements/theme-and-moral.md).

## Writing rules
- **FM.analogy.1.** Pick a familiar concrete thing whose workings truly match the idea.
- **FM.analogy.2.** Show or tell the situation before explaining what it means.
- **FM.analogy.3.** Keep the gap open long enough to build curiosity.
- **FM.analogy.4.** State the bridge clearly: "that's what happens when…".
- **FM.analogy.5.** Map only the parts that hold up; a forced analogy loses credibility. Say where the comparison stops, and never use it as factual proof beyond that point.
- **FM.analogy.6.** Close with a practical consequence or a change in perspective.

## Don't confuse with
- A quick metaphor: a passing comparison doesn't carry the format.
- [Everyday Conversation](everyday-conversation.md): having an everyday object isn't enough; you need the explicit abstract connection.
- Tutorial: explains how to use the object, not an idea through it.
