# Workflow: write a script

Read [behavior](../behavior.md), [the Floor](../floor.md) and [writing well](../writing-well.md) first. This is the path from a theme to a script ready to record. Each phase is a stopping point, and what is missing from one doesn't block the next. The creator can start at any phase. When you reach one, check what is missing from earlier phases, say in one sentence why it makes the video better and offer to do it together. If the creator wants to go on without it, go on without insisting.

## Two ways in

- **A direct request** ("write me a 45-second script about X"). Reuse the audience, topic, intended takeaway, objective, length and voice samples already provided. Ask at most two missing questions at a time; if a useful draft is possible, state your assumptions in one or two lines and write. A missing personal story does not block an explanatory script built from available facts. Choose a format the creator can actually produce, a structure and the strongest hook yourself, and say which you chose. Deliver one complete version unless the creator asked for alternatives, then offer the next step (hook options, a second version or a critique). Do not add an approval loop the creator didn't ask for.
- **The full path** (the creator wants to build the video step by step, or already has a video brief). Follow the phases below: the script starts from a chosen hook and style, and you write 2 or 3 versions (one, if the creator asks).

## The video brief

The video brief is the working record of one video. Keep it in the conversation and update it as decisions are made; with file tools and the creator's OK, save it to `sofia-english/briefs/<slug>.md` in the chosen folder ([files](../files.md)).

```
# {working title}
Theme: / Who it's for: / Bubble: {name} ({type})
Seed ({pillar}): {the sentence} / Common ground: / CTA: {hidden or direct}
Format: / Structure: / Addictive elements: {element: where}
Blocks: {one per structure step, short bullets tied to findings}
Research: key facts (with links), context, mistaken beliefs, curiosities, real cases,
quotes, bubble comments (literal, likes apart), Unverified
References: {links and templates}
Hook: {spoken line} / On-screen text: / Opening: / Recording style:
Versions: v1, v2, ... (chosen: vN)   Highlights: {numbered, with status}
Rejected: {ideas, hooks, styles not to bring back}
```

## Phase 0: the voice

Check for a saved voice ([creator voice](../creator-voice.md)). One saved voice: say "I'll write the way you talk" and follow its tone, rhythm, words and opening type. Several: ask which profile this script is for. None: offer to learn it once ([voice](voice.md)); if the creator declines, use [writing well](../writing-well.md) and plain American English, with natural contractions and no forced slang. Never require voice setup before writing.

## Phase 1: theme, bubble, research and idea

Follow [ideas](ideas.md) and [research](research.md). Before hooks and writing, check that the theme, the bubble and the audience's emotion are clear. If one is missing, say what, why it matters (it's what gets the video to people who care and holds whoever watches) and offer to do it together.

## Phase 2: format and structure

**Format.** Read the "How to choose" part of [formats](../formats/README.md) and the table matching archetype and format, and suggest 2 or 3 formats that fit the theme and what the creator can actually record:

> For this theme, these formats work best:
> 1. [Format]: [what it is, in one line]
> 2. [Format]: [what it is]
> Which one can you shoot?

Once the format is chosen, read its file. Its writing rules hold as law until the end. Read its analyzed example too, if there is one. The way of filming and editing (framing, cuts, captions) is the recording style, chosen with the hook.

**Archetype and structure.** Read [archetypes, niches and bubbles](../knowledge/archetypes-niches-bubbles.md) (the angle that already proved itself in the bubble) and [structures](../structures/README.md), and suggest 2 or 3 structures:

> From what you told me, I suggest these structures:
> 1. [Name]: [how it works, briefly]
> 2. [Name]: [how it works]
> Which do you prefer? I can write more than one to compare.

Format, structure and addictive elements must fit together. Read the structure's file and distribute the research findings across its blocks, in short bullets, with no spoken text yet. With a reference, the template guides the distribution. The creator approves before the hook.

## Phase 3: hook and recording style

Follow [hooks](hooks.md).

## Phase 4: the script

With a reference, ask first: **closer to the reference or more creative?** Close keeps the template's block order, rhythm and sentence type, with the variables filled by the creator's subject. Creative uses only the mechanism.

If something that changes the script is missing (the tone, a real case, the goal), ask first, at most two things. Before writing, confirm in a few lines when the path was step by step:

```
I'll write with:
Subject: {subject}
Format: {format}
Structure: {structure}
Hook: {chosen hook}
Tone: {how it will sound}
Is there a real case, number or example you want me to use?
```

Write only with real material. If a case is missing, ask: "For this part to work, I need a real example. Did something like this happen to you or to someone your audience knows?" In a direct draft, mark the gap as `[REAL FACT NEEDED]` and explain outside the spoken copy what is needed.

Write 2 or 3 versions (one, if the creator asks), each with a different structure or approach, each with a beginning, middle and end and one line saying its focus. Read [principles](../principles.md), [universal rules](../universal-rules.md), [addictive elements](../addictive-elements/README.md) and [writing well](../writing-well.md) once per conversation.

- **The slide.** The script opens with the bubble, slides through the common ground without the viewer noticing the change of subject, and closes with the commander's intent and a clear call to action ([breaking the bubble](../breaking-the-bubble.md)). The slide fits inside the structure: the opening is the hook, the transition happens in the middle, and the close brings the moral with the CTA.
- **The hold zone.** Right after the hook come 2 to 4 sentences, in the next 5 to 10 seconds, that confirm the subject the hook promised, deliver a concrete piece of it and show real proof with a source, or a concrete example the creator lived or told ([the hold zone](../hold-zone.md)). Invented proof stays out. Presence and production care go on the recording checklist.
- **The seed.** Each script plants the seed from the idea in the video brief, from one pillar of [commander's intent](../commanders-intent.md) (Authenticity, Movement or Product). Authenticity and Movement go inside the story, with no pause for promotion; the close reinforces a belief of the movement. The CTA is hidden in most videos, inside a story that happened, and direct in about 2 out of 10.
- **The comment in the script.** A well-liked bubble comment goes in as the creator's own line, said as theirs, where it fits best: in the opening, when it is a conflict or provocation comment with many likes (see [hooks](hooks.md)); in the middle, as proof, turn, example or reaction. No quotation marks, and the script never says it came from a comment: no "in the comments", "someone commented", "some people say", "a lot of people", "people", "followers" or "bubble". The words stay as close as possible to the original; change only what is needed for the sentence to make sense and be speakable, and drop emoji, links and @. Tell the creator in the conversation, never in the script, which comment and which video each line came from.

**Who writes and who reviews.** In Claude Code, the `scriptwriter` agent writes, and the full team reviews in parallel: `dont-do-reviewer`, `hook-reviewer` (only the hook and the hold zone), `voice-reviewer`, `bubble-specialist`, `structure-reviewer` and `fact-reviewer`. Without agents, run the same roles as passes ([passes](../passes.md)).

**The mechanical check.** If the Sofia tools are available in this session, run `check_script` on every finished version before delivering and check each point it returns in context (it can be a false positive, and it covers only part of the Floor); without them, check MC1 to MC16 by hand ([the Floor](../floor.md)). Then score [no AI-speak](../no-ai-speak.md): below 35 out of 50, rewrite.

### What each version carries

```
Version {n}: {title}. Structure {name}, focus on {dominant principle}
Format: {format} / Topic: {subject} / Takeaway: {the seed} / Estimated length: {seconds}
Hook: {only the spoken line of the chosen hook; it goes on the teleprompter}
How it opens: {what appears in the first 2 to 3 seconds and the on-screen text}
Script: {the full spoken text, block by block, with visual suggestions on separate lines}
Principles at work: {excerpt} / {principle} / {why}
Curiosity gaps: {gap} / {where it opens} / {where it closes}
What needs to be real: {what the creator needs to have lived, show or prove to record it}
```

Estimate the length at about 160 spoken words per minute, the same reference `check_script` uses for its estimate; it is a tool reference, not a rule of the method, and the creator's real pace decides. Keep sources and factual gaps outside the spoken copy. If the creator asks for clean spoken copy only, deliver that, with only essential source or missing-fact notes separated.

After the versions, say in two lines where the creator should pay attention, and list the sources used and, separately, what stayed "Unverified". When they choose, mark the chosen version in the video brief.

## Phase 5: choice and adjustments

Ask which version they prefer and whether they want to adjust something. Adjustments follow [review and rewrite](review.md): change only what was asked, show "before → after", keep each version, never cut or summarize without being asked.

When the creator adjusts the script their way (swapping a word, shortening a line, changing the tone), ask in one line whether to keep that as their way for future scripts. With their OK, remember it in the creator profile, with their own words.

If the creator edits the text themselves, their edit becomes a new version, marked as theirs: don't undo what they changed unless they ask. If they mark passages with their own notes, those are their requests, in their words; when they ask for the new version, carry each note into the text and save it as a new version.

## Phase 6: polish (the critique)

The critique is the default step after the script. When the creator chooses a version, say in one sentence why it's worth it (an outside eye catches what the writer doesn't see) and offer it:

> Now I'll run a critique on this script: I mark what to cut, replace or strengthen, and you accept or reject each mark.

Follow [critique](critique.md). If the creator wants to skip, respect it and go to recording. With the highlights answered, write the improved version from the accepted ones and keep it as a new version.

## Phase 7: recording

1. Check that the hook, the script and the chosen version match. If they don't, show the difference and ask.
2. Build the recording checklist for the chosen version: each scene with the spoken line, what to show and where to record, and at the end the continuous text for the teleprompter. The first scene is the hook: the spoken line, what appears in the first 2 to 3 seconds and the on-screen text. Add presence and production care (light, audio, framing) from [the hold zone](../hold-zone.md).
3. Never mark a video as recorded or posted unless the creator says so. Editing the recorded video is outside this plugin.

When the creator asks what to record first, order it like this:
1. News with an expiration date (newsjacking, or a script with a fact that changes). Say how many days have passed since the research. After 5 to 7 days, warn that it may be outdated and ask whether to record as is or update the facts first.
2. A script that ends in an invitation to comment or message, tied to a sales pain the creator is living now. Ask what the current pain is if you don't know.
3. Content with no deadline (educational, comparison), which can wait.

## After posting

Only when the creator asks. The numbers come from what the creator sends (pasted, exported or in a screenshot). Talk about the result against the profile's normal ("2x the profile's normal", "close to normal"). When the video worked, offer to add to the creator profile what is worth keeping (hooks, lines, styles, findings), showing the proposal first in two parts: **About the profile** and **To reuse**. Nothing is saved without their OK.

## Save and delete

- **Export:** the whole video brief in Markdown. With file tools, offer to save it to `sofia-english/briefs/<slug>.md`; without them, deliver the Markdown for the creator to copy.
- **Delete:** only when the creator asks. First say what will disappear and confirm.

## Rules for the whole writing process

- The theme is the most important decision. Validate it before anything else.
- Never invent a character, story, data or number. A real person only with a source you can check.
- A finding without a source stays under "Unverified" and out of the script.
- Write the way the creator talks: short sentences, action verbs, a specific detail in place of an adjective. "And", "So" and "But" can open a sentence.
- Use the language of the audience's bubble. Create familiarity and curiosity, without redundancy.
- The moral connects with the viewer's life.
- Follow the creator's saved voice. [The Floor](../floor.md) and [no AI-speak](../no-ai-speak.md) sit above everything: no filler -ly adverbs, no em dash, no "it's not this, it's that" contrast, no quotable line, no passive voice.
- Read it out loud in your head. If it doesn't sound natural, rewrite it.
- End with one relevant adjustment the creator can ask for.
