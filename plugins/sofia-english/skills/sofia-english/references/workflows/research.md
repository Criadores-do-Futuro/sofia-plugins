# Workflow: research

Read [behavior](../behavior.md) and [facts and sources](../facts-and-sources.md) first. The brainstorm has two kinds of research, with no fixed order between them: the subject (facts with sources) and the bubble (how the audience talks and what moves it). Sometimes they are the same.

Say in one sentence why it's worth it: the script gets good when it has sourced facts and the audience's own words. If the creator wants to skip, respect that and move on.

## The subject

1. **Check what you already have.** The video brief, the creator profile and the creator's saved files may already hold findings on this subject. Reuse what fits. A fact whose date is old gets checked again before it goes in.
2. **Ask what the creator trusts.** Sources they trust or reject, and the kind of finding they like to use. If they approve or reject a source, ask whether to remember that for future research (see [behavior](../behavior.md), learning from the creator).
3. **Search what is missing, in primary sources:** the document, the decision, the recorded statement, the official data. Look for key context, key facts, quotes, discoveries, common mistaken beliefs and current real cases.
4. **Mark each finding:** its type (confirmed fact, accusation, one side's version, opinion, hypothesis), how you reached the source (watched, read, screenshot, link only), the date of the fact and the caveat. A finding that corrects another marks the old one as replaced.
5. **Keep it in the video brief**, sorted into key facts, context, mistaken beliefs, curiosities, real cases, quotes and bubble comments, with the broad subject in a few words. What has no source goes under "Unverified" and stays out of the script.
6. **Deliver** with two lists at the end: "Sources" (with links) and "Unverified".

**In Claude Code**, the search goes to the `researcher` agent, with the subject and the claims to check. It uses the session's search tools, only researches and returns tagged findings; you keep them in the brief. **Without agents**, do the researcher's pass yourself ([passes](../passes.md)).

**With no way to search** (no web search and no research tool): say so in one sentence. A paid tool only if the person asks for one they already have, with their OK and a cost estimate. Otherwise, follow [manual research](../manual-research.md): what to search, where, which screenshots to take and how to send them. You read the screenshots, and each one becomes a comment saved exactly as written, a fact marked as coming from a screenshot with the date the screenshot shows, or an "Unverified" item. What gets saved goes into the proof in the hold zone, the bubble's words in the opening and the lines that came from comments.

## The bubble

1. **The bubble of this video.** See step 4 of [ideas](ideas.md). The bubble belongs to the creator's profile: what you learn about it serves every video that speaks to it, so offer to save it to the creator profile (with OK and file tools).
2. **The research.** Guide the creator on Instagram and TikTok, one step at a time, with [bubble research](../bubble-research.md): the most-liked comments (the method suggests 10 to 15 videos that went viral, as a guide, with no minimum), the vocabulary, what moves the bubble and the reference videos.
3. **Where it goes.** Comments go into the video brief exactly as written, without @ and without names, with the like count kept apart from the text. Vocabulary, what moves the bubble and the references go to the bubble's notes in the creator profile, with OK.
4. **Comments by screenshot.** TikTok comments need sign-in, so ask the creator to paste or export the most-liked ones, or to send screenshots of them. Transcribe each one exactly as written (slang, typos, caps, punctuation and emoji), without @ or names, and show the transcription for the creator to check against the screenshot before saving.
5. **The conversation.** Ask what caught their attention, what surprised them and what they agree with. That conversation often gives you the idea.

Sofia does not depend on another app: if data is missing, she asks for the file, the transcript, an export or screenshots. If the person asks her to use a tool they already have, she follows the request, after saying what it will fetch, how many items and the estimated cost.

## A reference video

If the creator brings a reference (a pasted transcript, a script or a link you can actually open), read it with [analyze](analyze.md) before suggesting any hook, and separate three things:

- **the author's parts:** names, cases, numbers, catchphrases, their personal story;
- **the mechanism:** the literal hook, the block structure and the principle activated in each block;
- **the template:** the text with the varying parts replaced by `{variables}`, such as `{niche}`, `{audience pain}`, `{verified number}`.

From the reference you copy the mechanism. The author's name, case, number and sentence stay with the author. A number from the reference without a source becomes a variable, never a fact. Save the template to the creator's swipe file only with their OK.
