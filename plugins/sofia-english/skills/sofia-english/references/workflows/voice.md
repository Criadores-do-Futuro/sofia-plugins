# Learn and apply the creator's voice

You learn how the creator talks so the scripts come out in their voice. The saved profile holds only a summary of the voice (tone, vocabulary, expressions, what they avoid). How the voice enters the text is in [creator voice](../creator-voice.md). Read [behavior](../behavior.md) first.

## When to offer

- At the start of a script, when there is no saved voice and personalization matters.
- In the first conversation with someone new.
- When the creator asks ("learn how I talk", "write it in my tone").

Do not force it before every script. Ask once, without pushing:

> Want me to learn how you talk? Send me 3 to 5 scripts, captions or transcripts from your best videos and I'll start writing in your tone. It takes a few minutes, and after that every script sounds like you. If you'd rather, I'll keep going without it for now.
>
> Do you always write for the same account, or do you have clients with different styles?

If they decline, continue with [writing well](../writing-well.md) and plain American English, and say they can ask for this anytime. A missing voice never blocks a script: use the sample in the request, if there is one.

### If they write for one account

1. Ask for 3 to 5 scripts they consider good.
2. Analyze them and build one voice.
3. Use that voice every time you write for them.

> Send me 3 to 5 of your scripts that you like. Paste them here or attach the file. I'll read them and learn how you write.

### If they write for different clients

Keep a separate profile per person or client, each with its own voice.

1. Ask which clients or accounts exist.
2. Ask for scripts from each client, separately.
3. Build one voice per client. Confirm the profile before saving.
4. Before writing, ask which client it is for, if it isn't clear.

> Who are your clients or accounts? Tell me the names and we'll keep the voices separate.
>
> (later) Now send me 3 to 5 scripts from {client}. I'll learn their voice separately.

## Step by step

1. **The handles.** Ask for the handle on each platform where they post. It is kept in the profile only as a reference. Do not try to log into the account or scrape the profile.
2. **The samples.** Ask for 3 to 5 texts from the videos that did best or that sound most like them, which the creator actually wrote or spoke. They can paste them or send a link you are able to open. If the link doesn't open, ask them to paste it. With fewer than 3 samples, say the summary will be shallower and explain the uncertainty.
3. **Read each sample quietly** and note what is in "What to look for in the scripts", below.
4. **Show the summary for them to confirm**, short, in bullet points. Check the summary against [no AI-speak](../no-ai-speak.md) before showing it:
   > Here's what I learned about how you talk. Does this sound right?
   Adjust whatever they correct.
5. **Ask what to avoid:**
   > Is there any word, tone or type of content I should never use in your scripts?
6. **Save only with their OK.** Show the profile before saving, unless they already asked you to save. When the host has file tools and the creator agrees, save it in their chosen folder at `sofia-english/styles/<profile-name>.md`: handles, niche, audience, topics, tone, vocabulary, expressions they use, what they avoid, and which samples the summary came from (one line per sample, with the title or topic, without the text). Never write inside the plugin installation. Confirm the path only after checking the file exists. Without file access, give the profile in the conversation in one block, ask them to keep it, and say it was not saved; next time they paste it and you use it.

## What to look for in the scripts

**1. Overall tone**
- Serious, playful, direct, provocative, reflective or relaxed.
- Speaks in first person (I) or third person (he, she, they).
- Goes straight in or builds context first.
- Uses humor, irony or provocation.

**2. Sentence rhythm**
- Sentence length: short, medium or long.
- Whether short and long alternate or follow one pattern.
- Whether they use incomplete sentences.
- How they pause and move from one idea to the next (the cadence).

**3. Words and expressions that repeat**
- Words that show up in more than one script, the way they write them.
- Expressions that are signature them.
- Slang and regional ways of talking.
- How they address the audience: you, you guys, y'all, everyone, fam.

**4. Opening**
- How they usually open: a strong statement, a number, a scene, a well-known reference.
- Whether they go straight to the topic or set the stage.

**5. Story order**
- How they organize the script: beginning, middle and end in a line, or back and forth (the structures they prefer).
- How much time they spend on the opening, the middle and the end.
- Whether they use lists, examples or stories.

**6. Ending**
- How they end: a lesson, a question for the comments, a call to action.
- Whether the ending is emotional or practical.

**7. What they avoid** (this weighs as much as what they do; absence is a pattern too)
- Kinds of sentences they never use.
- A tone they never take.
- A pattern they steer clear of.

## Voice summary template

```markdown
# Voice: {profile or client name}

## Tone
{2 or 3 sentences}

## Rhythm
- Sentence length: {short, medium or long}
- Pattern: {e.g., alternates short ones with one long one}
- Uses incomplete sentences: {yes or no}

## Words and expressions
- {the ones that show up most}

## How they address the audience
{e.g., "you", always casual, never "you guys"}

## Preferred opening
{e.g., strong statement, surprising number}

## Preferred story order
{e.g., opens with conflict, shows examples, closes with a lesson}

## Typical ending
{e.g., short lesson, no call to action}

## What they avoid
- {list}

## Reference lines
{3 to 5 lines from the scripts that show the voice well, for reference only. Never copy them into a new script.}
```

After saving, confirm:

> Done, I've learned your style. I saved it and I'll use it in your next scripts. Want me to write something now?

If it is per client:

> I saved {client}'s voice. When I write for them, I'll use it. Want to send scripts from another client now?

## Applying a saved voice

- One saved voice: use it without asking.
- Several plausible profiles: ask "Which account is this script for?" and list the names.
- None: offer to learn it once. Without an answer, write in an everyday conversational tone, with the bubble's words.

Match expression and rhythm without copying another person's story, identity or results. Facts always come first: a voice never justifies changing or inventing a fact.

## Rules

- The summary describes their voice. Do not copy whole sentences from the samples into the profile; a short expression they repeat can go in as vocabulary.
- Once saved, the voice applies to every script: tone, rhythm, words and type of opening. The [no AI-speak](../no-ai-speak.md) rules and the [Floor](../floor.md) still apply on top of the voice. If their scripts use something the Floor forbids, it does not go into the saved voice: say in one line that Sofia avoids that pattern and suggest another way.
- To update, repeat the steps with new samples and save again, preserving the earlier file unless they agree to replace it.
