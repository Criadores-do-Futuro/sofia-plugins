# Virality principles: the psychology of engagement

This page is the theory behind Sofia's method. These are observations the method drew from analyzing videos, with no scientific study cited. Present them to the creator as "the method observes", never as proven science. These are editorial tools, not predictions of views.

## The single goal

Every video sells an idea.

The product, the service and the profile come later. The idea is a belief the audience did not have, or had dormant, that the video activates, installs or reinforces.

If the idea is not clear before writing, the video has no center. Without a center, no mechanism works.

## What makes a video hold and spread

Retention is what allows distribution: without it, the video reaches no one. Retention alone does not make a video spread, though. What crosses bubbles and multiplies reach is what the mental debate makes a person do outside their head: share, comment, watch again.

## The hierarchy of the principles

The order below applies to the principles: it is the order in which you judge why a video holds or not. The addictive elements have their own order, in [addictive elements](addictive-elements/README.md), and it applies only when choosing the elements of a script. The names look alike, and each list serves a different job.

### 1. Theme

The theme sets the ceiling for everything. It is the size of the world of people this video can matter to, before any technique.

Universal themes (relationships, money, fear of failure, shame, injustice, belonging) arrive with the emotion ready. The video does not need to build the emotion, only wake it up. The more universal the theme, the less effort it takes to build and the higher the distribution ceiling.

Niche themes reach smaller groups with more intent. They work to hold and sell inside the bubble. They almost never cross bubbles on their own.

In the method's observation, a video with perfect technique and a small theme loses to a simple video with a universal theme. The theme is the first decision.

### 2. Familiarity

Before deciding whether to pay attention, a person measures how much effort it will take to understand. Familiarity lowers that effort. Without it, the audience gives up before engaging, without noticing they gave up.

Familiarity is recognition: situations already lived, emotions already felt, references everyone knows, beliefs already formed. Base it on the brief and the research; do not assume everyone shares the same culture or life experience.

Two kinds, with different jobs:

**Memory familiarity:** the audience recognizes the situation because they lived it. It creates identification. It holds inside the bubble.

**Identity familiarity:** the audience recognizes the belief, the value, the way of thinking as their own. It creates validation. People share it to be seen as someone who thinks that way. It crosses bubbles.

The strongest combination: familiar enough to get in without effort, new enough to be worth staying. The distance between the familiar and the new creates curiosity.

### 3. Conflict and change in the viewer's head

Conflict and change need to happen in the viewer's head. In the script, they may or may not appear on screen.

We notice changes, and what stays still goes unnoticed. Emotion is a turn: joy is the passage from neutral to happy. No turn, no emotion. No emotion, no retention.

The script causes this in three ways:

**Showing** conflict and change on screen: a situation that turns, a character who transforms, a value that goes from good to bad or from bad to good.

**Colliding** with a belief of the audience: contradicting what they took as true, breaking what they expected. The conflict happens between the video and the head of whoever is watching.

**Waking the memory** of a conflict the audience has already lived: the emotion exists before the video starts. The video only triggers what was already there.

In the method's observation, the third way is the strongest, because the video does not need to build anything. The more universal the conflict, the more ready-made emotion there is to wake up. Do not manufacture outrage or claim a belief is widespread without evidence.

### 4. Curiosity and mental debate

Curiosity is the distance between what the audience knows and what they want to know. While that distance exists, the person stays. When it closes, the urge to keep watching drops.

The question needs enough information to exist. The audience only gets curious about what they know they don't know. With too little information, they get confused. With too much, they are satisfied too early.

Mental debate is what the head does with curiosity: it imagines what comes next, compares it with its own life, forms an opinion, picks a side. That is real engagement. Likes, comments and shares come after it.

The more familiar the conflict that created the curiosity, the stronger the mental debate: the person has more memories to compare.

**Core rule:** the video that holds opens questions and keeps each one open for the right amount of time. A question closed too early kills the debate. In the method's observation, a question about a concrete fact (what happened, where, how much, with whom) holds more than an emotional question.

### 5. The aha and the question left open on purpose

The aha is the pleasure of discovering. It gets bigger the more the person thought before it. An aha delivered too early satisfies and does not hold.

Two paths:

**Deliver the aha** at the right moment, after the person has thought. They feel satisfied, share and watch again.

**Withhold the aha:** leave a question open on purpose. The person tries to resolve it outside the video: in the comments, in shares, in conversation. In the method's observation, this is the path that generates the most comments.

Withholding the aha is a choice: Sofia tells the creator she left the question open and why, and the hook's promise is still paid. A question forgotten without intent is bait (see the curiosity table in [addictive elements](addictive-elements/README.md)).

## How to diagnose a script

Before evaluating any technical element, answer in this order:

- **VP1.** **The idea this video sells.** Without a clear answer, the problem comes before any technique.
- **VP2.** **The theme and the size of the world it reaches.** This sets the ceiling before any analysis.
- **VP3.** **Where conflict and change happen**, on screen or in the viewer's head. If they happen nowhere, there is no emotion and no retention.
- **VP4.** **Where questions open and when they close.** A question that closes too early kills the debate. A concrete question left open on purpose generates comments.
- **VP5.** **Whether the aha arrives after the person has thought.** Too early satisfies without holding. A chosen absence takes the debate to the comments.

## Diagnosis rule

Never evaluate what is missing without weighing what is present.

A video can have imperfect technique and spread because the theme carries it. It can have a simple structure and hold because the conflict in the viewer's head is strong.

A useful diagnosis shows what is carrying the video and what could get bigger. Name a principle only when you can point to the words or images doing that work. A script does not need every principle in every beat.
