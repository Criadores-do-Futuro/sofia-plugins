# How to diagnose a script

Use this for a full critique, principle by principle. The foundation is in [principles](principles.md).

## The order of the diagnosis

Before judging any technique, answer in this order.

### 1. The idea the video sells

- It is the belief the video leaves in the viewer's head. It sits above the theme and the topic.
- If it doesn't fit in one clear sentence, the problem is in the structure, before any technique.
- Examples: "You can be great before you're recognized." "Hunger matters more than privilege."

### 2. The theme and the size of its world

- A theme that touches almost everyone (relationships, money, fear, injustice) has a high ceiling.
- A niche theme (digital marketing, running, gaming) has a lower ceiling.
- Check whether the theme arrives with emotion already in it or whether the video has to build that emotion.

### 3. Familiarity

- Check whether the viewer recognizes the situation because they lived it (memory familiarity).
- Check whether they recognize the belief as their own (identity familiarity). That is what makes people share outside the bubble.
- A name, term or reference the audience doesn't know raises the effort, and the viewer gives up.

### 4. Conflict and change

Check where they happen: on screen or in the viewer's head.

- On screen: the character changes, the situation turns, what was good goes bad.
- In the head: the video contradicts a belief or wakes up the memory of a conflict.
- If they happen nowhere, there is no emotion, and without emotion the viewer doesn't stay.

### 5. The questions and when they close

- List every question the video opens.
- Mark when each one closes: too early, at the right time or never.
- In the method's observation, a question about a concrete fact (what, where, how much, with whom) holds more than an emotional one.
- A question left open on purpose takes the conversation to the comments. A question forgotten, with no intent, is bait.

### 6. The aha

- Too early, it satisfies and doesn't hold.
- At the right time, after the viewer has thought about it, they share and rewatch.
- Left out on purpose, the viewer looks for the answer outside the video. Tell the creator that this was the choice and check that the hook's promise is still paid.

## The diagnosis rule

Never judge what is missing without weighing what is there. A useful diagnosis shows what is carrying the video and what could get bigger.

## Quick signals

Use these with the profile's numbers, when the creator shares them. Each line is a hypothesis to check. A number alone is not a conclusion: read the comments before deciding.

| Signal | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| The audience doesn't stop to watch | Hook with no question or no familiarity |
| They stop but leave quickly | Small theme or missing conflict |
| They watch it all but don't comment or share | No debate in their head, or an aha delivered before they thought about it |
| Many watch to the end, few share | Missing identity familiarity |
| Lots of comments | A question about a concrete fact left open, or controversy |
| Lots of shares | Identity familiarity and a strong moral |
